Portrait treatment  ·  pick one

Three ways to sit the photograph on the page

The backdrop of your headshot has been re-graded to the exact paper tone of the site, so in all three the seamless simply disappears — what differs is how much edge the photograph is allowed to keep.

A — Soft bleed

No frame at all. The photograph runs off the top and right of the page and dissolves into the paper on its two inner edges. The most editorial, and the most confident — it reads as part of the page rather than an element placed on it.

B — Arch

On the page now

A contained shape on the warm card tint. Quieter and more formal, and it borrows the arch motif that runs through a lot of craft and ceramics work. Safer, but it does put a frame between you and the reader.

C — Vignette

A soft circle with no hard edge, feathered all the way out. Warm and approachable, and the least demanding of space — but it shrinks you, and a round portrait reads more “team page” than “practice”.

What was done to the file
White point lifted, warm cast applied, then the seamless mapped onto #F4F2ED exactly. Saturation pulled back 6% so the denim doesn't fight the terracotta accent.
Why it needs no blend mode
Because the backdrop already is the page colour, the fades only have to soften the crop edge. No mix-blend-mode, which keeps it predictable in print and in Safari.
If you re-shoot
Shoot against a plain light seamless with even lighting and no vignette, and leave room above the head. Any backdrop can be re-graded to the paper tone the same way.
